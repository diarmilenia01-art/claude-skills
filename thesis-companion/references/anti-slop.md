# Anti-slop rules (academic-tuned)

Apply these ONLY to prose written for the reader (summaries, syntheses, paraphrases).
NEVER apply them to direct quotations, titles, citations, tables, or metadata —
editing a quotation falsifies the source.

## Design philosophy (read this first)

This is not Stop Slop copied in. It is Stop Slop **selected for a different goal.**
General-prose anti-slop optimizes for punchy, human, quotable writing. Academic
writing optimizes for something else: clarity, precision, and honesty.

So the selection principle is deliberate: **drop the most "anti-slop" rules — the
ones about trusting the reader, skipping justification, and varying rhythm — because
in academic writing they are counterproductive, and keep the rules that attack
vagueness**, which is the shared enemy of AI slop and bad scientific writing. A vague
claim with no evidence fails as prose and fails as scholarship for the same reason.

## Keep (transfers cleanly, improves academic writing)

- **Throat-clearing openers** — cut "It turns out", "The uncomfortable truth is",
  "Here's the thing", "Perlu dicatat bahwa". State the point.
- **Business jargon** — replace "navigate", "lean into", "deep dive", "game-changer"
  with plain words.
- **Meta-commentary** — cut "In this section we'll…", "As we'll see…", "Let me walk
  you through". Let the text move.
- **Vague declaratives** — replace "The implications are significant" / "The stakes
  are high" with the specific implication. This is the cardinal academic sin: a claim
  with no content.
- **False agency** — "the data tells us" → name who concludes ("we found",
  "the analysis shows"). Note: "the results show" / "hasil penelitian menunjukkan" is
  an accepted convention and stays; only the anthropomorphic "data speaks" style goes.
- **Performative emphasis** — cut "Let that sink in", "Make no mistake".
- **Rhetorical setups** — cut "What if I told you…", "Think about it". State the claim.

## Soften (adjust for academic register)

- **Passive voice** — Stop Slop bans it outright; academia does not. Keep passive when
  it is the disciplinary norm ("Samples were incubated for 24 hours") or the actor is
  irrelevant. Cut passive only when it hides an actor that matters ("It was decided
  that…" → who decided?).
- **Adverbs** — Stop Slop kills all; keep meaningful/technical ones ("significantly",
  "statistically", "approximately", "respectively"). Cut only empty intensifiers
  ("really", "truly", "deeply").
- **Absolutes (always/never/every)** — becomes a caution, not a ban. Prefer measured
  claims ("in this sample", "the majority"), but keep an absolute that is factually
  true.
- **Wh- sentence openers** — mostly restructure, BUT keep them in research questions
  ("How does X affect Y?") — that is the required form.

## Drop (do not apply to academic writing)

- **"Two items beat three"** — academic text often needs complete lists (variables,
  criteria, method steps). Completeness over rhythm.
- **"Ban em dashes / vary paragraph endings / no staccato / cut quotables"** — essay
  aesthetics, not scientific requirements. Downgrade to optional.
- **"Trust readers, skip justification"** — REVERSE this. Academia requires a citation
  for every substantive claim. Skipping justification is fatal here. The replacement
  rule: **every substantive claim must be supported by a citation, and paraphrased —
  never copied.**

## Scoring (academic version)

Rate 1–10; below 35/50, revise.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Clarity | Straight to the point, no throat-clearing? |
| Density | Anything cuttable without losing meaning? |
| Precision | Claims specific and measurable, not vague? |
| Academic honesty | Every claim cited? Paraphrased, not copied? |
| Scholarly tone | Formal and objective, but not robotic? |

Two original dimensions (Rhythm, "sounds human") are dropped in favor of Precision and
Academic honesty: sounding human is not the goal of a thesis; being clear, precise, and
honest is.
