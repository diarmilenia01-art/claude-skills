# Method (detailed reference)

Read for the labeling scheme and a worked example. Not needed for a quick scan.

## The three labels

- `kuat`, no objective red flag found on this point; it looks well-supported as presented.
 Use sparingly and honestly; absence of a flag is not proof of truth.
- `perlu dicek`, something to verify before believing it. This is where most findings
 land. It is a question, not an accusation.
- `rawan`, a clear objective trigger is present that often distorts (e.g. a truncated axis
 on a bar chart, a causal claim from observational data). Still phrased as a question plus
 the "when it is fine" case.

Bias toward `perlu dicek`. Reserve `rawan` for a concrete, hard-to-defend trigger.

## Stance

- The skill flags signals; the reader decides. It usually cannot see the raw data.
- It never says "this is false / a lie / manipulated". It says "here is what to check".
- If asked to give a verdict ("just tell me if it's true"), explain that it surfaces
 questions, not truth, and give the strongest questions instead.

## Worked example

Claim: "Our app boosts productivity by 200%. In a study, users finished tasks twice as fast."

- Label: perlu dicek
 Trigger: "200%" and "twice as fast" with no sample size or baseline.
 Question: how many users, over what tasks, compared to what baseline?
 When fine: if the study reports n, the control condition, and the task set.
- Label: rawan
 Trigger: a marketing claim citing "a study" with no source.
 Question: who ran it, was it independent, is it published?
 When fine: an independent, published study with methods available.
- Label: perlu dicek
 Trigger: causal framing ("boosts") from what may be a self-selected user group.
 Question: were users randomly assigned, or did motivated users self-select?
 When fine: a randomized design.

Closing line: "These are signals to check against the source, not proof of anything. The
call is yours, and I may be missing context I cannot see."

## Chart-image mode caveat

When a chart image is given, name the visual trigger (axis start, scale, dual axes,
missing labels) but state that image reading can miss detail, so every finding is a
question to confirm against the underlying data, not a conclusion.
